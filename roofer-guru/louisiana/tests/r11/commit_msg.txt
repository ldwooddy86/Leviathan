Louisiana Roofer Guru: storm scale, flying saucer and Tic Tac, more roof views

Module 18 of the Louisiana roofing atlas (Rooftop Scout) is renamed
Louisiana Roofer Guru, and the file with it:
uplift-louisiana-roofing-atlas.html -> louisiana-roofer-guru.html.

Scout Engine
- Moved to the DFW atlas's build 12 engine (the flicker and sharpness
  work), with Louisiana's roofs and roof views kept and build 12's
  heating and cooling inference left out.
- Storm scale, ten steps: dry, light rain, heavy rain, severe
  thunderstorm, tropical storm, hurricane Categories 1 to 5. Each step
  is a wind (the Saffir-Simpson ranges for the hurricanes) and a rain
  rate; drop size, fall speed, visibility and how level the rain flies
  follow from them. Rain, spray and debris are drawn on the GPU; wet
  surfaces take rings and runoff; trees lean; cloud races; lightning
  lights the scene, with thunder delayed by distance.
- From Category 3, within reach of the Gulf, flood water is drawn as an
  illustration of surge (old Saffir-Simpson heights, thinning inland to
  the distances Rita and Laura reached). It is labelled as a picture,
  not a forecast.
- The Alta X flies dry or in light rain only (Freefly rates it IP43).
  Past that it lands, will not take off, and the view says:
  "Don't waste a $40,000 drone, stupid. Use the UAPs."
- Two craft that fly in any weather: a classic domed flying saucer and
  the Tic Tac of the 2004 Nimitz encounter. Gravity warp takes either to
  an address or coordinates in Louisiana (elsewhere is refused); the
  saucer stands on its edge first, the Tic Tac does not.

Roof View
- Its own highlighted row, first in the bar, with seven choices.
- Covering: each roof's likely covering in its colour, with the
  covering's pattern drawn at real size (three tab, laminated, standing
  seam, ribbed panels, barrel tile, slate, membranes).
- Shingle style: three tab or laminated, from the roof's likely age and
  the share of each sold nationally that year.
- Storm: what the step on the storm scale would do to each roof, from
  the atlas's damage curve and FEMA-calibrated ratios; a judgment above
  Category 3, and said so.

Method, caveats and sources are extended for all of the above.
