Weather Desk

Roofing work follows the weather twice: rain, wind, fire and snow make the jobs, and dry, calm days let crews do them. This desk reads the National Weather Service forecast for one point in each region and the alerts in force for California, with the excessive rainfall and fire weather outlooks by county, and turns them into two clocks: a weather trigger index that marks the hours when rain, wind, fire weather, heat or snow press on roofs, which set the ads and bids for the days after, and a work window that marks the days crews can tear off, the days for repairs and inspections, and the days to stay off roofs. All times are Pacific.

READ THIS FIRST: THE WEATHER TRIGGER INDEX AND THE WORK WINDOW
The weather trigger index starts at 1.0 and adds for each sign of roof weather in the hourly forecast. Rain: 0.05 inch an hour or more adds the rate divided by 0.25 (up to 1.2), 1.5 inches in 24 hours adds 0.3 and 3 inches adds 0.6, and an atmospheric river named in the office's forecast discussion adds 0.2. Wind: gusts above 30 mph add one point for every 20 mph more, a High Wind Warning adds 0.5, and dry wind off the land (the Santa Ana, Diablo and sundowner pattern) adds 0.3. Fire weather: humidity of 15 percent or less with gusts of 25 mph adds 0.6, a Red Flag Warning over the point adds 0.8 and a Fire Weather Watch 0.4. Heat: 100 F adds 0.3 and 110 F adds 0.5. Snow: 0.5 inch an hour or more adds up to 1.0, 24 inches in 72 hours adds 0.5 and a Winter Storm Warning 0.3. Thunder, hail and a Flash Flood Warning add as well. Elevated is 1.4 or more and high is 2.0 or more; every weight is an assumption graded C. The work window marks daylight hours as good, marginal (rain chance of 25 to 49 percent, gusts of 22 mph or more, 95 F or more, frost, smoke, or a Red Flag Warning, when hot work stops) or blocked (rain chance of 50 percent or more, falling snow, thunder of 30 percent or more, or gusts of 30 mph or more); a day with four or more blocked hours is a day for no tear offs. Owners look for a roofer in the days after damage, so weather actions run after the window, never during it. Fire weather is different: it is a crew safety signal and a time for education, never for fear, and after a fire the disaster rules on price, licence and contracts apply (module 03).

Fire weather hours are forecast hours with humidity of 15 percent or less and gusts of 25 mph or more, or under a Red Flag Warning or Fire Weather Watch. The warning criteria themselves are set by each forecast office and are not in the evidence behind this atlas, so the desk reads the warning, not a rule of its own. C

WHAT TO DO WITH IT

A Red Flag Warning is a day for crew safety first: no hot work (torch applied membranes, kettles, grinding), vehicles off dry grass, and a plan to leave. Jobs in the warned zones should be closed up by the afternoon before. Do not advertise into fear while a fire burns. Hardening messages (Class A assembly, ember resistant vents, gutters and the first five feet) run as education in the warned zones only when no fire is burning there, and stop the moment an evacuation is ordered.

Triggers and playbooks

What a roofer does when each kind of trigger fires: before, during and after, with the California rules that bound the message.

Rain and atmospheric rivers · line Roof repair and leaks and Wind and rain damage C
Before	Two to three days ahead: stock tarps, sandbags for flat roof drains, sealant and pumps; clear gutters and scuppers on open jobs; dry in every open deck by the night before, or do not start the tear off. Call customers with roofs on the leak watch list.
During	Crews stay off wet roofs. Staff the phones for leak calls and book them in order; tarp only where it can be done safely. Leak repair ads stay on; replacement ads keep running.
After	For 72 hours after the window: leak repair and inspection ads in the ZIPs the rain covered, with the bid lift. Leak calls keep coming for days because owners find stains late. Re-roof enquiries follow in the weeks after a wet winter.
The rules	A contract to repair a home damaged in a declared emergency carries a seven business day right to cancel; the ordinary right is three business days, five for a senior (Business and Professions Code 7159). Negotiating a claim with the insurer for pay is public adjusting and needs that licence (Insurance Code 15006 and 15007). Write the estimate and the scope; do not offer to handle the claim. Never offer or advertise a discount meant to offset the deductible, and never pay an agent, broker or adjuster for a referral (Penal Code 551).

Each playbook is practice written for this atlas (grade C); the rules quoted are from the law register in module 03 and are checked against the copy by module 10. The seven day wait before soliciting in a reopened disaster area binds public adjusters by statute; the evidence holds no such statute for roofers, so the playbooks treat the reopening of the area as the floor for any outreach.

Rain season. Leak calls, tarps and emergency repairs. Tear offs need a dry window; book re-roofs for the dry months before it. Months from: atmospheric rivers by month (CW3E catalog).

Santa Ana season. Dry offshore wind in the south: lifted shingles and tile, and the highest fire danger of the year. Months from: Scripps Santa Ana winds regional index, mean days by month, 1948 to 2018.

Fire season. Hardening work sells before it; after a fire the disaster rules on price, licence and contracts apply. Months from: acres burned by month of ignition (CAL FIRE perimeters).

Heat season. Inland crews start at first light; shade above 80 F and high heat procedures at 95 F are the law for outdoor work. Months from: heat and excessive heat records by month.

Snow season. Mountain roofs: snow load, ice dams and short work days. Re-roofs wait for the melt. Months from: heavy snow and winter storm records by month.

.SYNOPSIS...Conditions will remain cool and cloudy as another, 
stronger storm system approaches Northwest California Monday. South 
winds are forecast to increase Monday afternoon with strong winds 
and heavy rain Tuesday alongside the potential for record breaking 
coastal inundation around high tide. 


&&

.DISCUSSION...Cool and cloudy conditions remain across the area with 
valley fog mostly inhibiting widespread valley frost early this 
morning. This system will bring increasing southerly winds gusting 
up to 25 mph by early afternoon with stronger winds through the 
evening hours. A few weak showers will stream along shore today with 
little expected rainfall. 

Strong winds will continue to increase into Tuesday as the front 
approaches. Models show that a low level jet of high winds 
(generally 55 to 65 kts) is expected over the Del Norte and Humboldt 
through Tuesday. These winds aloft have the potential to mix down to 
the surface, especially along the immediate coast from about Cape 
Mendocino and north into Oregon. The NBM suggest most likely wind 
gusts in exposed areas of Humboldt and Del Norte of just around 45 
mph. REFS ensemble shows a 70% chance of gusts at the coast of 45 
mph, and generally less than a 20% chance for 55 mph gusts. 
Deterministic models suggest some higher winds around Crescent City, 
but the need for a High Wind Warning remains marginal. Regardless, 
45 mph usually results in minor wind damage and small power 
outages. The potential for higher winds is generally less for most 
areas of Trinity, Mendocino and Lake counties. However, these areas 
will still most likely see gusts near 40 mph in exposed areas. Such 
speeds will be enough to increase the risk for power outages and 
significantly increase storm surge potential during the King Tides 
(refer to the 'Coastal Flooding' section for more info).

The higher rainfall potentials have decreased, but overall rain with 
the system will still be notable. NBM 24-hour rainfall totals (from 
Tuesday morning to Wednesday morning) indicates a 75 to 85 percent 
chance of 0.5 inches or greater for Del Norte, northern Humboldt and 
central Trinity (mostly along the Trinity River Valley). There is 
about a 65 to 85 percent chance for NBM 24-hour rainfall totals of 
1.5 inches or greater for most of southern Humboldt, Mendocino and 
northern and western Lake counties with closer to 0.5 to 1 inch 
expected for southeastern Lake. There is about a 60 to 80 percent 
chance of NBM 24-hour rainfall totals of 2 inches or more for the 
King Range in Humboldt County and the higher terrain areas of 
southwest Mendocino County. The greatest potential for heavy rain 
will be along the Mendocino Coast. Such rain amounts could cause 
minor to moderate small stream flooding, especially in streams that 
are tidally influenced. That said, CNRFC guidance shows rivers still 
have a ways to go before river flooding will be a concern. As a 
result, no significant river impacts are expected. The warm nature 
of the system will limit any snow concerns to above 5000 feet. 

Showers will persist early Wednesday alongside at least moderate 
south winds. Another cold front has the potential to pass by the 
region early Thursday. This front will bring another period of 
slightly weaker gusty southerly winds. Most ensemble models have 
generally weakened this front with only 0.2 to 0.6 inches of rain 
now most likely, all near shore north of Cape Mendocino. /JLW &

.MARINE...Southerlies continue to build overnight into Monday as an 
approaching cold front enter the area. A steep, mid-period swell 
will keep seas steep regardless of winds, lasting through Monday 
evening. Small craft advisories have been issued to cover for the 
hazards of this wave group before winds become the primary threat to 
mariners beginning early Monday afternoon. Widespread gale to storm 
force winds in the northern waters will build by late Monday night 
and peak during the day Tuesday. This will cause extremely chaotic 
seas and mariners are recommended to remain in port during this 
period. Warnings have been issued for each respective zone to cover 
the increasing threats from this weeks coming storms. 

Later this week after the initial storm passage, a moderate to large 
mid-period swell arrives to the waters at around 17-19ft @ 17sec. 
This swell will likely warrant a Hazardous Seas Warning, yet this 
will become more clear after more deep water buoy observations 
become available. Strong southerlies return Wednesday late morning 
lasting through Thursday early monring most likely promoting further 
gale warnings for the northern waters, yet confidence on coverage 
and timing remains low as of now. Expect additional NW swells and 
southerly winds through the coming week into next weekend as the 
storm track remains set over NW CA and the coastal waters.

.COASTAL FLOODING...High astronomical tides continue to grow 
this week peaking Tuesday through Thursday. Astronomical tides at 
the North Spit tide gauge reached 9.04ft Sunday(11/22) which is 
around 1.3ft above the standard modeled value of 7.7ft. The highest 
tides are expected in the late morning to midday hours through this 
period. 

The tidal anomaly has been reading consistently over 1.2-1.5 feet 
due to greater thermal expansion as warmer than normal ocean 
temperatures exist off the coastline due to El Nino. Storm surge 
from strong and persistent southerly winds and large breaking waves 
of 20-25 feet caused by the incoming storms will likely add an 
additional 1 to 1.5 ft Tuesday and Wednesday. These two factors 
(warm water + storm surge) have the potential to occur 
simultaneously, creating a total tide anomaly of 2.0 to 3.0 feet. 
Minor flooding around Humboldt Bay (water levels above 8.8 ft MLLW) 
is certain with moderate to major flooding likely during high tide 
each day through Friday of the coming work week. The highest 
astronomical tides for November are between 11/24 and 11/26 with 
each day reaching above 8.4 ft. Should the 1.5 ft anomaly remain, 
which is most likely, ~10 ft tide levels are expected. If the storm 
surge materializes as expected, the water levels could break the 
current record tide level of 10.37 ft MLLW set earlier this year in 
January. Storm surge is difficult to predict, but given the magnitude 
of winds on Tuesday, peak tides of 11 feet may be possible. 

Outside of Humboldt, the large swell expected late Tuesday into 
Wednesday could bring significant beach erosion and waves may wash 
over low lying areas of HWY 1 and HWY 101 along the coast at high 
tide. Impacts are also possible near Shelter Cove, Centerville 
Beach, and other exposed areas away from HWYs. 

A Coastal Flood Warning has been issued for Monday from 7am to 12pm 
around high tide in Humboldt Bay. Coastal Flood Watches have been 
issued for the entire NW CA coastline beginning Tuesday morning and 
lasting through Thursday's high tide. The time frame 2 hours before 
to 2 hours after high tide will very likely be switched to coastal 
flood warnings, especially for the Humboldt Bay area, during next 
week. Please prepare as best you can for this very likely record 
breaking event.

Method and sources
Forecast
api.weather.gov points, forecast, hourly forecast and raw gridpoint data (rain and snow amounts, wind gusts and direction, humidity, thunder probability and the weather attributes) for 11 points, and the active alerts for California with their zones. These are what Refresh pulls live.
Snapshot only
Station observations, the area forecast discussions of the ten offices (EKA, STO, MTR, HNX, LOX, SGX, MFR, REV, VEF, PSR), the fire weather outlook, the excessive rainfall outlook and the river forecast center discussion are read when the atlas is built and are not refreshed in the page.
Outlooks by county
The Storm Prediction Center fire weather outlook (days 1 and 2) and the Weather Prediction Center excessive rainfall outlook (days 1 to 3), each intersected with county boundaries: a county takes the highest level that covers part of it.
Atmospheric rivers
No machine readable forecast on the atmospheric river scale is public, so the desk reads rain amounts from the forecast grid and notes when an office names an atmospheric river in its discussion.
Trigger weights
Assumptions (grade C), printed in full in the callout at the top. A base of 1.0, elevated at 1.4, high at 2.0.
Work window
Common roofing practice: no tear offs with rain likely, snow falling, lightning nearby or gusts over 30 mph. The heat line is the state outdoor heat rule: shade above 80 F and high heat procedures at 95 F or more (Title 8, section 3395). Smoke is read from the forecast text and from air quality alerts; the desk does not read an air quality index (grade C).
