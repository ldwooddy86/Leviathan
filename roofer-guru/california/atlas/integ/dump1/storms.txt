Weather and Fire Ledger

Where weather and fire hit roofs. Every wildfire perimeter of 1,000 acres or more since 2000, and every fire that damaged a structure, is drawn on the map with the structures CAL FIRE's inspectors found destroyed and damaged. Atmospheric river landfalls sit on the coast by their rank on the AR1 to AR5 scale, Santa Ana, Diablo, sundowner and winter wind events at the place of their strongest measured gust, and every flood, debris flow, hail, wind and tornado report that carries coordinates at its own point. Type a ZIP to read what has touched it. This is the view for response: which ZIPs to work after an event, when each kind of weather peaks, and what the record says each does to a roof.

READ THIS FIRST: A RECORD IS NOT A DAMAGED ROOF
Four kinds of record are on this map and each is read differently. A fire perimeter is the mapped edge of a burn; the structure counts inside it are CAL FIRE damage inspections, which begin in 2013 and from 2018 also list the buildings that survived. An atmospheric river landfall is ranked by the water vapor it carried across the coast, not by the rain that fell on a town. A wind event is a set of forecast zone records; the gust shown is the strongest one measured, often on a ridge or in a canyon, not at rooftops in town. A located report is a place where someone or an instrument recorded flood, debris flow, hail, wind or a tornado. Most California weather is filed by forecast zone with no coordinates (high wind, heavy snow, heat, most wildfire records), so the dots are thin exactly where the weather matters most: use the ZIP shading and the ZIP lookup for those. Use the record to place an event and size the roof market it left, and never as proof that a given roof was damaged.

What the ranks, zones and thresholds on this tab mean, and where each comes from.

PEAK WATER VAPOR TRANSPORT (KG PER METRE PER SECOND)	UNDER 24 HOURS	24 TO 48 HOURS	OVER 48 HOURS
250 to 500	not an AR	AR1	AR2
500 to 750	AR1	AR2	AR3
750 to 1,000	AR2	AR3	AR4

Ralph and others, 2019 (Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society), as used by the Center for Western Weather and Water Extremes. The rank comes from the strongest three hour water vapor transport and how long it stays at 250 or more at one point on the coast: under 24 hours drops the rank by one, over 48 hours raises it by one. AR1: weak, primarily beneficial. AR2: moderate, mostly beneficial, also hazardous. AR3: strong, a balance of beneficial and hazardous. AR4: extreme, mostly hazardous, also beneficial. AR5: exceptional, primarily hazardous. It ranks the storm at the coast, not the rain at a house. A

DAMAGE INSPECTION LEVELS

CAL FIRE inspectors class each structure in or within 300 feet of a perimeter: destroyed (more than 50 percent), major (25 to 50), minor (10 to 25), affected (up to 10) or no damage. This tab calls the middle three "damaged". Digital records begin in 2013; undamaged structures are recorded from 2018. A

FIRE HAZARD SEVERITY ZONES

The State Fire Marshal maps hazard, not risk, in three classes: Moderate, High and Very High. The map for land the state protects took effect on April 1, 2024; the map for land cities and counties protect is the Marshal's recommendation of March 24, 2025, which each city or county adopts by ordinance and may enlarge, so the legal local map can differ. Federal land is in neither map. In the overlay behind this atlas 61,392 square km are Very High, 39,178 High and 27,448 Moderate (45% of the land in ZIP areas). For a roofer the zones decide the roof: in a zone a new roof, or a re-roof of half or more of the roof within a year, must be a Class A assembly (module 03 has the code sections). The shares shown per ZIP are land area, not homes. A

RED FLAG WARNINGS

A Red Flag Warning is the National Weather Service's notice that wind, low humidity and dry fuels make fire spread likely. Each forecast office sets its own thresholds; none is in the evidence behind this atlas, so no criteria are printed here and the Weather Desk (module 14) reads the warnings themselves. D

WIND AND HAIL SIZES

Wind records here are classed at 40, 58 and 75 mph. In forecasters' own narratives roof damage begins to appear at about 60 to 70 mph; that is a reading of the record, and no California threshold by roof covering is in the evidence. Hail is classed at 1 inch, where asphalt shingles begin to bruise, and 1.75 inch. Tornadoes carry the Enhanced Fujita rating from the damage survey. C

Wildfire	The Palisades and Eaton fires of January 2025 destroyed 6,845 and 9,419 structures in the inspections tied to their perimeters here; CAL FIRE's final incident counts are 6,845 and 9,419. More than 95 percent of the structures in those two fires were built before the 2008 wildfire building code, and about nine in ten already had Class A roof coverings: a Class A covering is the best available and did not save homes on its own. Embers enter at vents, eaves, gutters full of debris and gaps at the roof edge. In older fires a wood roof was the strongest single predictor of loss (Painted Cave, 1990: 70 percent of homes with non flammable roofs survived against 19 percent with flammable ones).	A
Offshore and winter wind	The strongest gust in a named offshore event on this ledger is 130 mph (Santa Ana, Nov 19, 2023), measured on high ground. In the event narratives, roofs are damaged from about 60 to 70 mph: lifted and missing shingles, slipped and broken tile, ridge caps and flashing. No insurer or utility count of California wind claims was found, and no threshold by covering is in the evidence.	C
Rain and atmospheric rivers	Rain does not break a sound roof; it finds worn underlayment, flashing and flat roof drains. Los Angeles issued 40% more re-roof permits in 2023 than in 2022, after the wet winter of 2022 to 2023 (10,383 against 14,511); that the rain caused it is a judgement, and it is the best demand signal found. Tile roofs leak when the underlayment under the tile wears out, at about 25 years, which is the tile lift and relay line. No count of roof leak claims is published.	C
Flash flood and debris flow	Flood damage is not roof damage. Debris flows below burn scars destroy whole buildings in short, heavy rain; 205 located records here name a burn scar. For a roofer they are a crew safety matter and a reason to keep ads free of flood imagery.	B
Hail	Rare. 287 located reports of hail of 1 inch or more are on this map for the whole state, 1996 to 2026. Hail is not a roofing market in California; inspect on request after a report nearby and say so plainly.	A
Mountain snow	125 ZIPs carry a design ground snow load of 100 pounds per square foot or more at the town, and the highest ZIP value is 995. In the winter of 2023 roofs collapsed in Crestline, Lake Arrowhead and Nevada City after a series of storms. The work is snow removal, ice dam and leak repair, and metal roofing; whether a structure can carry its load is an engineer's question.	B
Heat, sun and salt air	Inland heat limits the work day (shade above 80 F and high heat procedures at 95 F for outdoor work) and the Energy Code asks for reflective products on many re-roofs in the hot zones. How fast shingles and coatings age in California heat, and how far salt air shortens the life of metal near the coast, are not in the evidence: the salt and marine layer classes per ZIP are judgement rules. From event to campaign

How the record turns into pages, placements and crews, with the California rules that bound each use.

Fire pages	One page per fire and the towns it reached, published only after the area reopens and updated as inspections finish. Lead with CAL FIRE's facts, then what smoke and ember damage looks like on a standing roof, what a rebuild roof must be in a fire zone, how to check a licence, and the owner's rights. Open a fire above for a ready brief. Never publish into an evacuated area and never use fear.	A
After a declaration	A federal, state or local emergency declaration switches on the disaster rules: repair and reconstruction prices no more than 10 percent above your own price from before it, for 180 days, and building materials for 30 days (Penal Code 396); a seven business day right to cancel on every contract to repair the damage (Business and Professions Code 7159); the maximum fine for breaking the down payment and progress payment limits (7159.5); and unlicensed contracting that can be charged as a felony (7028.16). Keep a dated price book from before the season.	A
Insurance claims	Write the estimate and the scope and explain the construction to the adjuster if asked. Do not negotiate the claim for the owner for pay: that is public adjusting and needs its own licence (Insurance Code 15006 and 15007). Never offer or advertise a discount meant to offset the deductible, and never pay an agent, broker or adjuster for a referral (Penal Code 551).	A
After an atmospheric river	Leak repair and inspection messages in the ZIPs under the landfall for the 72 hours after it, and tarps and dry in before it; module 14 watches the forecast. Use the ZIP's own rain days and the coast class here to choose where: far inland the rank at the coast says little.	B
After a wind event	Repair and inspection messages where gusts of 58 mph or more were measured, in the forecast zones the event names. Offer inspections, not outcomes. Dry offshore wind is also fire weather: crews do no torch work and the message is never fear.	B
Canvassing and contracts	Home improvement contracts over $500 must be in writing with the notices the law lists; the buyer may cancel within three business days, five for a senior, seven for disaster repair; the down payment is $1,000 or 10 percent, whichever is less. A person who sells at the door needs a home improvement salesperson registration (module 03).	A
Who you compete with	5,587 active C-39 roofing licences are on the state roll, 170 of 5,829 with an out of state mailing address. After a declared disaster the licence is the message: unlicensed work is where the law bites hardest, and the licence number belongs on every ad, truck and contract. Module 13 holds the licence field by city.	A
What never to publish	The address, photo or name of a specific damaged home or owner without written permission; "free roof", "we pay your deductible", "we handle your claim" or "insurance approved"; a perimeter, a landfall or a gust presented as proof that a particular roof was damaged; any promise that a roof will survive a wildfire or keep an insurer. Caveats

What would change a conclusion drawn from this map.

Most weather has no point	High wind, heavy snow, winter storm, heat and most wildfire records are filed by forecast zone, with no coordinates. They are in the yearly and monthly series and in the ZIP columns, not among the dots.
Airport gusts are screened	Daily peak gusts from airports contain sensor spikes of hundreds of miles an hour. A gust is kept only when it is under 2.25 times the fastest sustained wind of the day and passes two more tests; counts at 75 mph remain the least reliable, and the nearest screened station can be far from a ZIP (see the quality letter).
Gust reports come from high ground	Measured gust reports are dominated by ridge, canyon and summit sensors. A 100 mph gust on a truck trail is not a 100 mph wind on a subdivision.
Inspection categories changed in 2018	Before 2018 only damaged and destroyed structures were recorded, and roofs were classed only as fire resistant, combustible or unknown. Shares by roof covering are shown for 2018 and later only, and are raw.
Fires before 2013 have no structure count	The digital inspection records begin in 2013. The Cedar, Witch and other large earlier fires are on the map with acres and no count; none was invented.
Perimeters are incomplete	CAL FIRE says so: some fires are missing or duplicated, old outlines are generalized, and each spring adds the previous year, so fires of the current year are not yet in.
Atmospheric river ranks are at the coast	A ZIP takes the record of its latitude on the coast. Inland, in the lee of the Sierra and in the deserts, far less arrives; use the rain columns for what falls at the ZIP.
Named winds are lower bounds	An event counts as Santa Ana, Diablo or sundowner only when the record names the wind. The Santa Ana index stops in 2018 and no catalog exists for the other two.
Damage dollars are estimates	Property damage in the federal storm records is the forecaster's estimate and is blank in most rows.
Zone shares are land, not homes	Fire Hazard Severity Zone shares are the share of the ZIP area. They understate exposure where homes cluster at the wildland edge of a large ZIP.
Method and sources

How fires, landfalls, wind events and reports were built.

Fires are CAL FIRE historic perimeters of 1,000 acres or more since 2000, plus every perimeter or incident with a damaged structure; each inspected structure is tied to the perimeter of its incident by name and distance, so the ledger's totals equal the inspection file's. Atmospheric river landfalls are the CW3E catalog at the California coast points, merged along the coast into one storm with its highest rank. Wind events group the federal High Wind and Strong Wind zone records by kind and overlapping dates. Located reports are federal Storm Events records with coordinates and National Weather Service Local Storm Reports, placed in ZIPs and counties by their coordinates. ZIP values are taken at the town in each ZIP from the nearest stations, with elevation counted as distance. Nothing on this tab is modeled damage: every count is a record.
