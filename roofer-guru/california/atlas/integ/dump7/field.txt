MODULE 07 · COMPETITIVE FIELD · THE LICENSE ROLL, ROOFING ESTABLISHMENTS AND NAMED COMPANIES
Competitive Field

California roofing is a long tail with no public ranking. 5,587 active C-39 Roofing licenses stand against 2,926 roofing establishments with employees, about 1.9 licenses for each: many licensees have no payroll, and no public file says how big any of them is. So this module reads the field from what is counted: every licensed roofer by the ZIP and city where the license gets its mail, the establishments the Census Bureau counts, a pressure surface by ZIP built from both, and the ground where licensed roofers are few. The 4 named companies on record are only those a public source names; they are not a roster of the market.

READ THIS FIRST: THREE COUNTS, THREE DENOMINATORS
License records (5,587 active and unsuspended C-39 Roofing licenses in the Contractors State License Board's master file) show who may legally sell roofing: a license is needed for any job of $1,000 or more (Business and Professions Code 7048). A license is counted where it gets its mail, which is where the firm is, not where it works. Roofing contractors (2,926 establishments with employees in County Business Patterns, NAICS 238160, and 17,116 nonemployer firms in the exterior trades) measure the trade with a payroll. Named companies (4) are only those a public source names; fields nobody read on a company's own site are blank and flagged. Competitive pressure blends licenses near a ZIP (60%, weighted by distance) with roofing establishments (40%). None of this measures size or ad spend; module 13 watches the ads themselves.

What the roll says about the trade

Read from the license file and the Census counts.

Mostly small	2,926 roofing establishments have employees (23,903 workers, about 8 each); the license roll is about 1.9 times as long, so roughly half the licenses belong to firms with no payroll or to firms whose main trade is something else.
Specialists	3,933 of 5,829 licenses on the roll (67%) hold C-39 and nothing else; the rest pair roofing with other classes.
Workers' compensation	A C-39 licensee must keep a workers' compensation certificate on file even with no employees, and the insurer must audit the payroll in person every year (Business and Professions Code 7125; Insurance Code 11665). It is the commonest reason a roofing license is suspended.
Bond	Every licensee carries a $25,000 contractor's bond (Business and Professions Code 7071.6).
Sales staff	Anyone who solicits or signs home improvement contracts for a licensee must be a registered home improvement salesperson (Business and Professions Code 7152).

Certifications, financing and Spanish pages were not read from company sites for this edition, so nothing is said about them. Module 13 is where to read a competitor's own pages and ads.

What the field means for a campaign

How to read pressure, licenses and thin ground.

Crowded and open counties	Active C-39 licenses per 10,000 residents run highest in Mono (6.8), Alpine (6.2) and Mendocino (3.7) and lowest in Imperial (0.4), Kings (0.3) and Inyo (0.0). A high rate can mean a crowded market or a county whose roofers work the next one over: read it with the pressure surface.
Thin ground	168 scored ZIPs have 3 or fewer licensed roofers within 25 km, most of them in Far North (41), San Joaquin Valley (37) and North Coast (22). Job flow there is smaller per ZIP and the drive is long, but a roofer with a local address has few rivals, and in fire country the work is specialized.
Spanish	0 of the named companies show Spanish pages. Los Angeles (1.01M), San Diego (258k), San Jose (212k) and Bakersfield (209k) hold the most residents who speak Spanish at home. The Campaign Desk builds Spanish only where limited English is common, with the license number in Spanish.
Thin lines	Roof replacement (0 companies), Roof repair and leaks (0 companies), Wind and rain damage (0 companies) are marketed by the fewest named companies. With so few companies read, treat this as a hint.
Pressure is not price	The pressure surface feeds the cost model in module 05; actual auction prices arrive with account data (module 15). After a large storm or fire, crews and advertisers arrive from other counties and prices jump for weeks.
Judgment calls and sources

How the counts were built and what they miss.

License counts	Every license in the CSLB master file whose classifications include C-39, counted at the mailing ZIP and city on the license. Active means status clear. The file lists renewed licenses only, so the roll is not everyone who ever held the class.
Within 25 km	Licenses are placed at the center of their mailing ZIP and counted within 25 km of each ZIP's center. The distance was carried over from the first edition; in a large metro nearly every ZIP has many, which is why the thin field is defined by the lowest tenth and not by zero.
Named companies	4 companies are listed, only those a public source names. 0 were read on their own sites. A ranked California roster could not be built honestly: no public file measures size.
Offices	Offices of named companies sit at the city or ZIP center, not the street address.
Missing	Size, revenue, crews, reviews and ad spend of any roofer. Unlicensed operators are, by definition, not on the roll.
