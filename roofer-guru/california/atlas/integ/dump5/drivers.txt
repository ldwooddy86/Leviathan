MODULE 04 · DEMAND DRIVERS · 1,483 ZIPS
Demand Drivers

Why California roofs are replaced on a clock set by the builder and not by the weather, why the same winter that floods the phones with leaks pays for almost no roofs through insurance, why an insurer's letter now sells more roofs than a storm does in the foothills, why roofers cluster in cities while the fire zones sit in the hills, and when in the year the work arrives. This module tests the obvious explanations against observed outcomes with a weighted regression across ZIPs, projects the age engine ten years out with tile on its own clock, reads the seasons from the rain, wind and fire record, sizes the market from the Economic Census and the license roll, and records how every California column was built.

READ THIS FIRST: FIVE FINDINGS THAT SHOULD CHANGE A MEDIA PLAN
1. Wear, not weather, replaces California roofs. No storm resets the stock here. Owners in the Los Angeles metro replaced 9.8% of roofs in two years, San Francisco and Oakland 7.6%, San Jose 7.3%, Riverside and San Bernardino 5.9% (American Housing Survey), and the timing follows the decade the tract was built. 2. Insurance pays for almost none of it. Insurance paid for 2.0% of roof jobs in Los Angeles, 1.3% in San Francisco, 0.0% in San Jose and 8.4% in Riverside, against 41% in the west south central states. Claim funded copy fits few jobs; financing and price do the work. 3. Insurance still drives demand, from the other side. FAIR Plan home policies went from 265,909 to 665,673 between September 2022 and 2026. An owner who is non-renewed, or who wants off the FAIR Plan, is asked about the age and fire class of the roof: a deadline no ad could create. 4. Tile runs on a second clock. The tile itself outlasts the house; the underlayment does not. The tracts of the 1980s and 1990s are due now, and the job is a lift and relay, not a new roof. 5. The year has one wet season. 83% of the normal rain falls November to March. Leak and storm calls arrive then; reroofs, tile and coatings are sold then and built in the dry months.

What this means for a roofing marketer

The findings translated into placements, pages, offers and crews.

Sell the clock, not the storm	Replacement demand follows the decade a tract was built. Fund replacement and tile campaigns in the ZIPs where roofs aged 15 to 19 and tile underlayment past 25 years concentrate (module 17), with inspections and financing as the offer. Nobody is waiting for an adjuster.
Use the wet season twice	From November to March leak and storm calls arrive on their own: answer fast, repair, and document the roof. The same visit is the best lead for a dry season reroof or relay. Budget emergency search for the winter and planned purchase search for late winter through summer.
Meet the insurance letter	In fire zone and high FAIR Plan ZIPs the buying moment is a non-renewal notice or an inspection. A page that explains what an insurer looks for in a roof (age, Class A, vents, gutters), a fast written report, and the fire hardened roof line catch owners at that moment. Describe the process; do not interpret anyone's policy or promise a discount.
Tile is its own business	Lift and relay needs crews who can handle tile without breaking a third of it, and copy that says the tile stays. Aim it at the 1980s and 1990s tracts of the Inland Empire, Orange County, the Sacramento suburbs and the Central Valley, where the model puts the most tile past 25 years.
Go where the roofers are not	Licenses follow population, not fire zones or rain. Foothill and mountain ZIPs with old roofs and few local licenses are the opening for a regional company with a local address and a license number on every ad; module 07 maps the field.
After a fire, slow down	Rebuild roofing runs for years, under a seven business day right to cancel, a 10 percent price cap for 180 days after the declaration and felony exposure for unlicensed work in the disaster area. Build pages and relationships with builders, not a tent in the parking lot.
Build record: how California's columns were made, and what was found on the way

Every choice in the data build that would have changed a number, and every assumption with no evidence behind it.

Which ZIPs are in	One row per populated 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Area whose largest land share is in California and where the ACS shows one resident or more: 1,762 rows. Areas that are mostly Nevada or Oregon are out. Model columns are blank under 500 residents (1,531 ZIPs are scored).
County of a ZIP	A ZIP that crosses a county line is given to the county with its largest land share; the data keeps the number of counties each ZIP touches.
ACS medians the Census cannot estimate	Published as large negative codes; set to missing before any average, rate or regression.
One unit homes by decade	The roof model needs one unit homes by decade built. The ACS publishes them in six wide year bands by tenure; each band was split into decades in proportion to all homes by decade in the same ZIP, then scaled to the one unit total.
Roof covering	No file records what covers each roof. Tile, wood and metal shares are the 2020 Residential Energy Consumption Survey's California sample by era built (837 single family homes), with a climate factor by region for tile, rescaled to the survey's statewide shares. Grade D.
Two roof clocks	Roofs other than tile wear on a Weibull life with a mean of 24.5 years (shape 3.00), refitted to the American Housing Survey West region replacement rates by year built, net of the jobs insurance paid for. Tile wears on the life of its underlayment (mean 27.0 years), after which the same tile goes back. For a tile roof every age in the atlas is the age of the underlayment.
No storm reset	The first edition of this model reset roofs after every major storm. California's has one reset only: structures CAL FIRE recorded as destroyed leave the stock in the fire year and re enter as new roofs over the next four years.
FEMA's roof flag	FEMA's roof damage field is empty for every registration from 2020 on. Verified real property loss without flood damage stands in from 2020; the COVID declaration is left out everywhere. Owner and insurance counts in the yearly table are counted on all registrations, not only the damaged ones.
Non-renewals	The Department of Insurance file for 2020 to 2023 does not separate the insurer's decision from the owner's; the 2015 to 2021 file does, so insurer initiated non-renewals stop at 2021.
Licenses	C-39 licenses are counted at their mailing ZIP, whole numbers, active and unsuspended. Competition near a ZIP counts licenses within 25 km and a distance weighted sum. The first edition counted named company offices; California has no public list of roofers by size.
Business Patterns	ZIP cells under three establishments are withheld, so small towns read zero roofers; postal ZIPs that are not tabulation areas are counted in county totals only. State totals use the county file.
Weather columns	Rain, heat and gust days come from the nearest stations to each ZIP's town point, with distance weighted for elevation difference and for distance from the coast, because a coastal station says little about a canyon ten miles inland.
Zero rates	A California ZIP can have a rate of zero on the fire line; 0.01 is added before the logarithm so the index can still be computed.
Housing survey reach	The survey samples Los Angeles, San Francisco and Riverside in 2023 and San Jose in 2021. San Diego and Sacramento take the Pacific division figures.
Assumption 1	Jobs open to marketing (REP) for every line: the model's original defaults carried over for the lines it already had; fire 0.60, harden 0.85, tile 0.84, solar 0.50 are new and chosen by judgment. No California measurement exists.

Assumption 3	Gross margin 33% (the model's original default; the 2022 Economic Census shows materials at 34% and payroll at 26% of California roofing revenue).
Assumption 4	Regional click cost factors (DMA): Los Angeles and Ventura, Orange County and the Bay Area 1.00; San Diego and Imperial 0.90; Inland Empire 0.85; Sacramento Valley and Central Coast 0.80; San Joaquin Valley 0.65; North Coast and Sierra 0.60; Far North 0.55. No measured California click costs were available.
Assumption 5	Media market names per region (MKT) are typed from general knowledge by principal city, not from a Nielsen county list.
Assumption 6	Weibull shape 3 for both life curves (the model's original value). Tile underlayment mean life 27 years (two trade sources say about 25 and 20 to 30; owners defer).
Assumption 7	The calibration target is the AHS West region rate by year built less 9.5% paid by insurance, with California's tile shares by era; the West region is not California alone.
Assumption 8	Tile and wood shares by ZIP come from the statewide RECS 2020 shares by era built and a two way climate factor (hot dry 1.13, marine 0.67; mountains given the marine factor). No ZIP level roof material source exists.
Assumption 9	A reroofed home keeps its kind of covering (tile stays tile).
Assumption 10	Wildfire: 40% of destroyed homes are rebuilt within four years (Census homes built 2020 or later in ZIPs that lost 200 or more residences in 2017 to 2020 equal 22% of the residences destroyed); 50% of fire damaged homes get a new roof the next year.
Assumption 11	Metal is 4% of residential reroofs statewide (2.2% of today's stock, RECS), 1.6 times in ZIPs under 300 residents per square mile, 0.6 times over 1,500, 1.5 times where a quarter of homes are rated high wildfire risk or snow load is 40 psf or more.
Assumption 12	Fire hardened roof line: 2% a year of wildfire exposed owner homes (the larger of insurer rated High or Extreme homeowners exposures and FAIR Plan owner occupied policies). 70% of it is taken out of the replacement line, since a hardened roof is usually the reroof done early.
Assumption 13	Storm line: the state total is AHS Pacific insurance paid share (3.5%) times the AHS Pacific roof job rate (3.67% of homes a year) times one unit roofs, times 3 for repairs short of replacement (the multiplier is a guess). It is spread over ZIPs by a weather index: 0.5 + 0.3 per 58 mph gust day + 1.2 per 75 mph gust day + 0.3 per AR3 or stronger day + 0.2 per day of 2 inches of rain, a year (weights by judgment).
Assumption 14	Fire line: the rebuild roofs due in the next twelve months from the fires of the last four years (a quarter of the rebuilt share each year) and half of last year's fire damaged homes, plus half the 2013 to 2025 state yearly mean spread over ZIPs by wildfire exposed homes (fires not yet lit). The halves are by judgment.
Assumption 15	Solar detach and reset: every reroof or tile relay on a home with solar needs one; the share of homes with solar is the same at every roof age.
Assumption 16	Solar in ZIPs served by city or district utilities (Los Angeles DWP, SMUD and others) is not in the state interconnection file; those ZIPs take the county's share from covered ZIPs or the older Project Sunroof count, whichever is larger (column solar_src = "est").
Assumption 17	Mobile homes: AHS West 4.15% a year with 46% done by the owner (22 sampled costs); wind and rain damage at the site built rate.
Assumption 18	Multifamily buildings are estimated at 7, 14, 30 and 80 homes a building by ACS size class and replaced on a 22 year life; commercial roofs also 22 years (carried from the model's original assumptions).
Assumption 19	Commercial roofs: 5,918,000 U.S. commercial buildings (Energy Information Administration, Commercial Buildings Energy Consumption Survey 2018, Table B1, revised December 2022; read 2026-11-23) times California's share of U.S. residents, spread by establishments weighted by building type. The national count is the survey's; giving California its share of residents, and the weights by building type, are assumptions: the survey publishes no California count.
Assumption 20	Coatings: 3% of commercial roofs a year (the model's original assumption).
Assumption 21	Roof leak rates by decade built are the AHS Southern profile by decade rescaled so the California stock averages the AHS Pacific 5.25%; 55% fixed by a paid roofer.
Assumption 22	A ZIP belongs to the county holding the largest share of its land.
Judgment calls and sources

Choices that move the results, and where the data came from.

Weights	ZIPs are weighted by homes so a small ZIP's noisy rate counts less.
Logs	Outcomes, density and income are logged because they are skewed; ZIPs with a zero outcome are set to half the smallest observed rate. Coefficients are in standard deviations.
Observed outcomes only	The modeled job counts are not used as outcomes here, because they are built from these same predictors; FAIR Plan policies, non-renewals, FEMA registrations and licenses are observed.
Missing predictor values	A ZIP with one missing predictor takes the mean for that predictor, so it is not dropped.
Ecology	Associations between ZIP averages do not describe individual homeowners.
Typed figures	The Economic Census payroll, materials, subcontract and national totals and the west south central insurance share are typed from the evidence file, with their table named beside them. Roofing receipts, establishments and employees, and every other figure on this page, are computed from the embedded data.
