California Regions

California is not one roofing market. The same re-roof is a tile lift and relay in a hot inland subdivision, a Class A assembly with ember resistant vents in a foothill fire zone, a snow load question in the mountains and a leak call on the wet north coast. Each region here is a group of whole counties with one climate, one housing stock and, as far as counties allow, one set of media markets. The tab sums the county tables and weights the ZIP columns for each region: people and homes, roof stock and age, rain, wind, heat, snow and fire, the insurance retreat (FAIR Plan policies and non-renewals), roofing licences and employers, and the languages to build for.

READ THIS FIRST: HOW TO READ THE REGIONS
Each region sums its counties. Roof ages come from the roof age model (module 17); in California the model ages roofs with no storm resets, and only wildfire puts new roofs on whole neighborhoods at once. Weather, fire zone and insurance measures are ZIP columns averaged with homes as the weight, so a region's figure describes where its homes are, not its empty land. Today Sacramento Valley has the oldest roofs (28% at 20 years or older), Inland Empire the largest share reaching 20 within five years (17%), Inland Empire the most tile (41% of one unit roofs), North Coast the most rain (45 inches in a normal year), Sacramento Valley the most structures lost to wildfire on record (23,257), Sierra the most FAIR Plan policies per 100 homes (35.6). A region card is the brief for a regional campaign: which lines run hot, how old the roofs are, what the weather and the fire maps do to them, how far insurers have pulled back, who is licensed to compete, and which languages to build for. Every figure is computed from the atlas data; where a column is missing the measure is left out.

How this tab is built

Sums, weights and the region to market mapping.

The 11 regions are groups of whole counties chosen for this atlas by climate, housing and media market; they are not a state agency's scheme. Roof stock, roof ages, replacements and rebuild roofs sum the county tables of the roof age model (module 17); FEMA registrations, licences, employers and languages sum by county; model pools sum the scored ZIPs, each assigned to the county holding most of its land. Weather, fire zone, tile and insurance rate measures are ZIP columns averaged with housing units as the weight (one unit roofs for the roof shares); structures destroyed are ZIP sums. FAIR Plan and non-renewal totals come from the insurance rollup by region when the data carries it, which counts every postal ZIP the source places in the county. A region's roofing character lists the measures on which it stands first or second among the regions, so it changes when the data does. A measure whose column is missing from the data is left out of every table.
