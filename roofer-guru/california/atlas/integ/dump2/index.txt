MODULE 01 · ROOF DEMAND INDEX · CALIFORNIA, 58 COUNTIES
Roof Demand Index

Every roofing job in California starts with one of four things: a roof that wore out, weather that found its weak point, a fire, or a building that is new. And more and more often an insurer's letter sets the date. This module estimates where those jobs arise for thirteen residential and commercial service lines, from the age of every home, what covers it, the rain, wind and wildfire record, the insurance files and FEMA's damage registrations, and resolves them to the ZIP codes where the roofs are. The map below is the master heatmap for ads and SEO; every other tab reads from it.

READ THIS FIRST: WHAT THE INDEX IS AND IS NOT
This maps where roofing jobs arise, not where they are sold. No agency counts roof replacements by ZIP, and California has no statewide permit file: whether a reroof needs a permit depends on the city. What can be counted is everything around the roof: the homes and the decade each was built (Census), what the 2020 federal energy survey says covers California homes of each era, the rain, wind and heat at the nearest stations and the atmospheric river record (NOAA, CW3E), every structure CAL FIRE inspected after a wildfire, the fire hazard zones, FAIR Plan policies and insurer non-renewals by ZIP (the FAIR Plan and the Department of Insurance), FEMA's registrations for every California disaster since 2003, the commercial buildings (Business Patterns), new permits (Census) and every C-39 roofing license (CSLB). A roof cohort model ages every roof, fitted to the American Housing Survey, with tile on its own clock. The replacement, repair, tile and new construction lines rest on observed housing data (grade B or C); storm, fire, hardening and the commercial and specialty lines lean on stated assumptions (grades C and D) that you can change. A high index is a ZIP where more roofing jobs arise per resident; module 05 turns that into reachable jobs against competition, module 02 shows the weather and the fires, and module 17 the roofs themselves.

How the index is built

Thirteen components, one per service line. Each weight is the line's share of the statewide gross profit pool at default assumptions times a confidence tier. Every number in the chain is shown.

For each line the expected jobs per 10,000 residents are shrunk toward the county rate for small ZIPs (an empirical Bayes prior worth 2,500 residents), log transformed (a small constant is added first, because a California ZIP can have a rate of zero on the fire line), standardized across the 1,531 scored ZIPs (a z score), multiplied by the weight, summed and turned into a percentile. Weight is the line's share of the statewide gross profit pool at default assumptions times a tier: 1.0 where the line rests on observed counts (grades A and B), 0.75 for graded models (C), 0.5 for lines anchored on an assumption (D). Money stands in for importance because a roofer's choice between lines is a choice between job values; that substitution is the largest judgment call in the build.

JUDGMENT CALL: WEAR, WEATHER, FIRE AND INSURANCE ARE SEPARATE LINES
A roof replaced because it wore out (retail, financing, price shopping), one repaired after an atmospheric river (urgent, small, rarely a claim), one rebuilt after a wildfire (insurer led, slow, regulated) and one upgraded to Class A because an insurer or the FAIR Plan asked (a deadline and a discount) are different sales. The cohort model feeds the replacement and tile lines; the weather record spreads the storm line; CAL FIRE's inspections feed the fire line; insurer wildfire ratings and FAIR Plan policies feed the hardening line.
JUDGMENT CALL: NO DOUBLE COUNTING
Tile relays are counted only in the tile line and never as replacements. Metal reroofs and most hardening reroofs are carved out of the replacement line. Solar detach and reset is a job beside a reroof, counted once as its own line. Mobile homes are only in their own line, and buildings of five units or more sit in the multifamily line. Repairs count leaks fixed by a paid roofer, not every leak.

Fire: agreement between CAL FIRE's inspections and FEMA's registrations is weak (ρ = 0.27). The fire line is built on the inspections, so this says the inspections point where households also reported loss; it does not test the rebuild share. Storm: agreement is weak (ρ = 0.17). That is not a pass. California has no storm that leaves a clean roof damage record the way a landfalling storm does elsewhere, so the storm line stays an exposure weighted spread of a state total (grade C at best) and should be corrected with your own job history. The state totals of the storm, fire and hardening lines rest on anchors listed in module 12, each with its grade.
Caveats: the fine print

Things that would change a conclusion drawn from this map.

Jobs, not sales	Every layer describes roofs, weather and buildings, not signed contracts. No agency counts roof replacements by place.
Ecological reading only	A high ZIP contains old roofs and careful owners; nothing here describes a household.
ACS is a survey	ZIP estimates carry margins of error, largest in small rural ZIPs; the index shrinks small ZIP rates toward the county rate for that reason.
The roof life is fitted, not observed	The Weibull life (mean 25 years for coverings other than tile) is fitted to the West region's 2023 American Housing Survey replacement rates by year built. California specific roof ages are not published, and the tile underlayment life (27 years) rests on one consultant's planning figure.
Roof covering is modeled	The tile, wood and metal shares come from a national survey's California sample by era built (837 single family homes), not from a count of roofs. A ZIP of Spanish revival homes from the 1920s will carry more tile than the model gives it.
Insurance pays for few California roofs	In the American Housing Survey, insurance paid for 2.0% of roof jobs in the Los Angeles metro and 3.5% across the Pacific division, against 41 percent in the west south central states. Storm and fire lines are therefore small beside wear, and claim funded copy fits few jobs.
Fire records start in 2013	CAL FIRE's damage inspection file covers fires since 2013 that the agency inspected. Older fires and some local agency fires are missing, so "none on record" is not "never burned".
Default job values are placeholders	The gross profit pool multiplies jobs by default values (replacement $19,000, commercial $75,000 and so on) and a 33% margin. Replace them with your closed job history in module 12.
Research material	Not legal or insurance advice. Verify statutes and program rules against the primary source before relying on them.
What this atlas cannot show

Documented gaps. Each says why, so the next request goes to the right custodian.

Roof permits by ZIP	There is no statewide permit file. Whether a reroof needs a permit depends on the city, and each city keeps its own system; Los Angeles and San Francisco publish theirs and are used as checks only.	C
Insurance claims for roofs	The Department of Insurance publishes fire and smoke claims by ZIP, not wind or water claims on roofs. The share of roofing jobs paid by insurance comes from a housing survey, for metros and the Pacific division only.	C
Search volume and CPC by ZIP	No keyword or Ahrefs data was pulled for this build. Cost per click is modeled from region and competition (grade D) until account actuals arrive in module 15.	D
Roof material by home	No public source records roof covering by parcel; tile, wood and metal are modeled from era built and climate (module 17).	D
Rooftop solar by ZIP	Carried as a ZIP share; its source and vintage are in the data build record (module 04).	C
Commercial roof age and size	Assessor files that carry building area and year are not published statewide; the commercial lines run on establishment counts and a national building count.	D
How big each roofer is	The CSLB file lists every C-39 license with its city and ZIP but no measure of size, and no ranked list of California roofers by revenue is public. Competition is counted in licenses and employer establishments.	C
San Diego and Sacramento in the housing survey	The American Housing Survey samples Los Angeles, San Francisco, Riverside and (in 2021) San Jose. San Diego and Sacramento are not sampled on their own; the Pacific division figures stand in.	B
