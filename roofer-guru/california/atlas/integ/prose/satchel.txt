License. The CSLB license lookup confirms the company, its classifications (C-39 Roofing, or a class that covers the work) and its status. A license is needed for any job of $1,000 or more, and the number belongs in every ad.

Bond and workers' compensation. Every licensee files a $25,000 bond, and a C-39 roofer keeps a workers' compensation certificate on file even with no employees; ask for the certificate.

Every rule the batteries apply. ✔ marks a rule whose text was read for this build (November 2026), on a primary page, a dated archive capture or a transcription host. ◐ marks a secondary source, a platform policy or our own reading: read the live text before it appears in a confirmed finding.

A block level rule with its primary text read (✔) and a match in observable text is CONFIRMED. A ◐ rule, or any warning, is a CANDIDATE until the open question is closed. Notes are INTEL. Surfaces this page cannot see are NOT_OBSERVABLE. Controls move candidates to CLEARED.

Copy rules run sentence by sentence and are counted per source; the license statements run once per asset set, using the Company panel in self audit and the observable text in competitor posture; web tests read the raw source for pixels, prechecked boxes, self served ratings, the license number, the office city and promises hidden in meta tags and schema. A pasted contract runs the home improvement contract tests (heading and license number, the copy statement, the down payment sentence, the progress payment warning, the Mechanics Lien Warning, the right to cancel notice and email address, and any down payment above $1,000); a pasted door to door or call script runs the salesperson, right to cancel and consent tests.

In self audit posture, the house rules block any hyphen or dash and any meta note or placeholder in copy. They are off in competitor posture: style is not a violation.

California has no roofing statute on claim advertising. What it has is the public adjuster license: negotiating or settling a claim for an insured for pay, or advertising that, needs it. So an offer to negotiate or handle the claim blocks, while softer wording such as "claims specialists" or "we work with your insurance" is a candidate, because no text read says how far a roofer may go in showing damage to an adjuster. Deductible offers block outright under Penal Code 551.

"Fireproof" blocks as a candidate for review, never as a confirmed finding, because the basis is the meaning of a Class A rating and the fire evidence, not a statute that names the word. A discount size, a promise of renewal and a promised grant are candidates: Safer from Wildfires sets no percentage, the FAIR Plan publishes no figure for the roof alone, and grant amounts were not set in the sources read.

A competitor ad with no license number is a candidate, not a finding: the license may exist and the number may be elsewhere. The CSLB license lookup settles it, and unlicensed advertising is the board's to act on.

The contract tests look for the statute's own words. A contract negotiated at the contractor's place of business needs no cancellation notice, a bonded job is outside the down payment cap, and jobs of $500 or less and commercial jobs are outside the statute, so the cancellation and down payment rows are candidates, not confirmed findings.

"Best" and "#1" need a verifiable basis; a cited award may substantiate them, so they never auto confirm. California's false advertising statutes were not read for this build.

The copy rules read English. Spanish, Chinese, Vietnamese, Tagalog and Korean ads and pages need a reader of that language for the claim, deductible, fire and warranty language.
