More law touches a California roofer than a roofer almost anywhere else. The license line is $1,000 and the license number goes on every ad. A residential job over $500 needs the state's written contract, a down payment capped at $1,000 or 10 percent, and a right to cancel of three, five or seven business days. Roofers alone must carry workers' compensation with no employees. After a declared disaster prices are capped for 180 days and unlicensed work can be a felony. In a fire zone, replacing half a roof brings the Class A new roof rules; across most of the state, replacing more than half brings the cool roof rules. Insurers work to a 15, 40 and 30 day clock and must weigh a Class A roof in the rate, at no set percentage. Each item below carries its citation, its source and a grade, and the calculators turn the rules into answers for one job.

The license number is the ad	It must be in all advertising, so put the CSLB number on every ad, page, truck, sign and door hanger, and say "check our license". It is the fastest answer to out of area crews after a fire or a storm, and the first thing the board looks for.

Sell the contract terms	The down payment cap, the right to cancel by email and the written warranty are consumer protections most owners do not know. "No big deposit" and "three days to change your mind" are lawful, true and hard for an unlicensed seller to match.

No claims talk	"We handle your claim" and "we negotiate with your insurer" describe public adjusting, which needs its own license. Say what a roofer does: inspect, document damage with photos, write an itemized estimate and scope, and do the work.

Deductibles	"We pay your deductible", "deductible assistance" and credits sized to the deductible are barred by Penal Code 551. Financing the owner's share is the lawful answer to a large deductible, with Regulation Z disclosures.

Fire: the assembly, not a promise	Say "Class A fire rated roof assembly" and name it. Never "fireproof". Most roofs inside the January 2025 fire perimeters were already Class A; vents, eaves, gutters and the first five feet decide as much, so sell the roof edge with the roof.

Insurance pressure	With 698,307 FAIR Plan policies and non-renewal notices arriving 75 days ahead, owners in fire zones are looking for what an insurer wants to see. A page on what Safer from Wildfires lists, with "ask your insurer for the figure", meets them honestly. Never a discount size, never a promise of renewal.

Cool roofs	Past 50 percent of the roof, most inland and southern climate zones need rated reflective products. "We check your climate zone and file the rating sheet" is a claim a careless competitor cannot make. Most tile is exempt by weight.

Tile	The tile era homes of 1980 to 1999 are past the underlayment life two practitioners give. "Your tile is fine, the paper under it is not" explains the job; the code lets sound tile go back.

After a declaration	Prices stay within 10 percent for 180 days, repair contracts carry seven days to cancel, and every crew needs the California license. A dated price book and a local address are the proof.

At the door	A registered salesperson who names the contractor and license first, the right notice for the buyer, and no talk of claims or deductibles. Local solicitation permits were not researched.

License · Bus. & Prof. Code 7026, 7048 (Stats. 2024, ch. 240, AB 2622; Stats. 2025, ch. 67, AB 1170); Cal. Code Regs. tit. 16, 832.39 · $1,000 line from January 1, 2025 (it was $500) · All lines

Anyone who offers, bids or does work to build, alter, repair or improve a structure is a contractor, subcontractors included. The only exemption is minor work: the total price for labor, materials and everything else is under $1,000, and the work needs no building permit, and the person employs no one. Splitting a job into smaller contracts to stay under the line is not allowed. The roofing class is C-39: it covers installing and repairing surfaces that seal, waterproof and weatherproof structures, from shingles, tile, slate and shakes to metal roofing systems, urethane foam and membranes. In practice almost every reroof needs a licensed contractor, because the price is over $1,000 and most cities require a permit (that last sentence is our reading, grade D). What a roofer must do: hold an active C-39 (or another class that covers the work) before offering or bidding any roofing job.

License · Bus. & Prof. Code 7030.5; 7027.1 and 7027.2 (Stats. 2024, ch. 240, AB 2622) · In force; 7027.2 as amended January 1, 2025 · All lines

Every licensee must include the license number in all construction contracts, in subcontracts and calls for bid, and in all forms of advertising, as the registrar prescribes. Advertising as a contractor without a license is a misdemeanor with a fine of $700 to $1,000 on top of any other punishment. An unlicensed person may advertise only for jobs under $1,000 and only if the ad says the person is not licensed. What a roofer must do: print the CSLB license number on every ad, page, truck, sign, door hanger, estimate and contract. Section 7030.5 was read on a transcription host, so it is graded B; sections 7027.1 and 7027.2 were read as archive captures of the Legislature's pages.

Every applicant and licensee keeps a contractor's bond of $25,000 on file with the Contractors State License Board. Claimants other than homeowners and employees are capped at $7,500 in aggregate; the rest is reserved for the homeowner and employee beneficiaries. A license qualified by a responsible managing employee, or by an officer who owns less than 10 percent, also needs a $25,000 bond of qualifying individual. After a revocation the board may require a disciplinary bond of at least $25,000 and up to ten times the contractor's bond for at least two years. No general liability minimum was found in the sections read; a home improvement contract must instead say whether the contractor carries commercial general liability insurance.

Liens · Civil Code 8200, 8204, 8412, 8414, 8416, 8460; Bus. & Prof. Code 7159(e)(4), 7159.5(a)(6) · In force (operative July 1, 2012) · All lines

Subcontractors and suppliers must give a preliminary notice to the owner, the direct contractor and any construction lender no later than 20 days after first furnishing work; a late notice protects only work from 20 days before it was served. Disaster · Penal Code 396(b), (c), (g), (h) · On any federal, state or local emergency declaration · Fire, Storm, Repair, Replace, Tile relay, Fire hardening, Mobile home, Multifamily, Commercial

For 180 days after an emergency is proclaimed or declared, a contractor may not sell or offer repair or reconstruction services, or emergency cleanup services, for more than 10 percent above the price that contractor charged immediately before the declaration. Claims · Insurance Code 15006, 15007; People v. Montgomery-Sansome, LP (San Mateo County Superior Court No. 17CIV04659) · In force · Storm, Fire, Repair, Replace, Tile relay, Multifamily, Commercial

A public insurance adjuster is a person who, for compensation, acts for or aids an insured in negotiating or settling a property claim, or who advertises or holds out as an adjuster of those claims. Doing that without the license carries a civil penalty up to $10,000, or $25,000 if willful. A San Mateo County trial court applied the Act to a restoration contractor that offered to negotiate with insurers at no cost in return for the repair job: $1,250 per violation, $56,250 in total, and an injunction against negotiating or advocating for a homeowner with an insurer. That is one trial court decision, read on a law firm's site (grade C); it does not bind other courts and an appeal was not checked. What a contractor may safely do, by our reading only (grade D, no statute or agency notice draws the line): write an estimate and scope of work, give it to the owner, and meet the adjuster to explain the construction facts.

It is unlawful for a contractor, or its employees or agents, to knowingly offer or give any discount intended to offset a deductible required by a policy covering repairs to a residential or commercial structure. The one exception: the insurer has determined the amount of the claim and the work is done in accordance with that determination or with estimates the insurer accepted. Advertising a discount is allowed only on the same condition. It is also unlawful to offer an insurance agent, broker or adjuster any fee, commission or other consideration for referring an insured. Where more than $950 is at issue the offense is punishable by 16 months, two years or three years, a fine up to $10,000, or both, or by up to one year in county jail; otherwise by up to six months, $1,000, or both. Unlike some states, no bold contract notice about the deductible was found in California law.

The search found no California statute specific to roofing or repair assignments. That is an absence in the search, not proof there is none. The general rule read is Insurance Code 520: a policy clause that forbids transferring a claim after the loss is void if made before the loss. An assignment does not make claim negotiation by an unlicensed person lawful (see the public adjuster row).

Consequential damage from making the repair is part of the loss, and the insured pays only the deductible. The expense of labor to repair, rebuild or replace is not a component of physical depreciation; only materials normally replaced during a building's life are depreciated, and each adjustment must be itemized in dollars. Actual cash value is the cost to repair, rebuild or replace less fair physical depreciation, and depreciation applies only to components normally replaced during the structure's life. On a replacement cost policy the insurer pays actual cash value first and the difference once the property is repaired or replaced.
