Roofing work follows the weather twice: rain, wind, fire and snow make the jobs, and dry, calm days let crews do them. This desk reads the National Weather Service forecast for one point in each region and the alerts in force for California, with the excessive rainfall and fire weather outlooks by county, and turns them into two clocks: a weather trigger index that marks the hours when rain, wind, fire weather, heat or snow press on roofs, which set the ads and bids for the days after, and a work window that marks the days crews can tear off, the days for repairs and inspections, and the days to stay off roofs. All times are Pacific.

The weather trigger index starts at 1.0 and adds for each sign of roof weather in the hourly forecast. Rain: 0.05 inch an hour or more adds the rate divided by 0.25 (up to 1.2), 1.5 inches in 24 hours adds 0.3 and 3 inches adds 0.6, and an atmospheric river named in the office's forecast discussion adds 0.2. Wind: gusts above 30 mph add one point for every 20 mph more, a High Wind Warning adds 0.5, and dry wind off the land (the Santa Ana, Diablo and sundowner pattern) adds 0.3. Fire weather: humidity of 15 percent or less with gusts of 25 mph adds 0.6, a Red Flag Warning over the point adds 0.8 and a Fire Weather Watch 0.4. Heat: 100 F adds 0.3 and 110 F adds 0.5. Snow: 0.5 inch an hour or more adds up to 1.0, 24 inches in 72 hours adds 0.5 and a Winter Storm Warning 0.3. Thunder, hail and a Flash Flood Warning add as well. Elevated is 1.4 or more and high is 2.0 or more; every weight is an assumption graded C. The work window marks daylight hours as good, marginal (rain chance of 25 to 49 percent, gusts of 22 mph or more, 95 F or more, frost, smoke, or a Red Flag Warning, when hot work stops) or blocked (rain chance of 50 percent or more, falling snow, thunder of 30 percent or more, or gusts of 30 mph or more); a day with four or more blocked hours is a day for no tear offs. Owners look for a roofer in the days after damage, so weather actions run after the window, never during it. Fire weather is different: it is a crew safety signal and a time for education, never for fear, and after a fire the disaster rules on price, license and contracts apply (module 03).

During	Crews stay off wet roofs. Staff the phones for leak calls and book them in order; tarp only where it can be done safely. Leak repair ads stay on; replacement ads keep running.

After	For 72 hours after the window: leak repair and inspection ads in the ZIPs the rain covered, with the bid lift. Leak calls keep coming for days because owners find stains late. Re-roof enquiries follow in the weeks after a wet winter.

The rules	A contract to repair a home damaged in a declared emergency carries a seven business day right to cancel; the ordinary right is three business days, five for a senior (Business and Professions Code 7159). Negotiating a claim with the insurer for pay is public adjusting and needs that license (Insurance Code 15006 and 15007). Write the estimate and the scope; do not offer to handle the claim. Never offer or advertise a discount meant to offset the deductible, and never pay an agent, broker or adjuster for a referral (Penal Code 551).

Each playbook is practice written for this atlas (grade C); the rules quoted are from the law register in module 03 and are checked against the copy by module 10. The seven day wait before soliciting in a reopened disaster area binds public adjusters by statute; the evidence holds no such statute for roofers, so the playbooks treat the reopening of the area as the floor for any outreach.

Rain season. Leak calls, tarps and emergency repairs. Tear offs need a dry window; book re-roofs for the dry months before it. Months from: atmospheric rivers by month (CW3E catalog).

Santa Ana season. Dry offshore wind in the south: lifted shingles and tile, and the highest fire danger of the year. Months from: Scripps Santa Ana winds regional index, mean days by month, 1948 to 2018.

Fire season. Hardening work sells before it; after a fire the disaster rules on price, license and contracts apply. Months from: acres burned by month of ignition (CAL FIRE perimeters).

Heat season. Inland crews start at first light; shade above 80 F and high heat procedures at 95 F are the law for outdoor work. Months from: heat and excessive heat records by month.

Snow season. Mountain roofs: snow load, ice dams and short work days. Re-roofs wait for the melt. Months from: heavy snow and winter storm records by month.

A season's months are the fewest months that hold 80 percent of its record, except Santa Ana months, which are those with three or more Santa Ana days in an average year.
