California is not one roofing market. The same re-roof is a tile lift and relay in a hot inland subdivision, a Class A assembly with ember resistant vents in a foothill fire zone, a snow load question in the mountains and a leak call on the wet north coast. Each region here is a group of whole counties with one climate, one housing stock and, as far as counties allow, one set of media markets. The tab sums the county tables and weights the ZIP columns for each region: people and homes, roof stock and age, rain, wind, heat, snow and fire, the insurance retreat (FAIR Plan policies and non-renewals), roofing licenses and employers, and the languages to build for.

Each region sums its counties. Roof ages come from the roof age model (module 17); in California the model ages roofs with no storm resets, and only wildfire puts new roofs on whole neighborhoods at once. Weather, fire zone and insurance measures are ZIP columns averaged with homes as the weight, so a region's figure describes where its homes are, not its empty land. Today Sacramento Valley has the oldest roofs (28% at 20 years or older), Inland Empire the largest share reaching 20 within five years (17%), Inland Empire the most tile (41% of one unit roofs), North Coast the most rain (45 inches in a normal year), Sacramento Valley the most structures lost to wildfire on record (23,257), Sierra the most FAIR Plan policies per 100 homes (35.6). A region card is the brief for a regional campaign: which lines run hot, how old the roofs are, what the weather and the fire maps do to them, how far insurers have pulled back, who is licensed to compete, and which languages to build for. Every figure is computed from the atlas data; where a column is missing the measure is left out.

Each column is shaded on its own scale across the 11 regions. Rain, heat and gust days come from the nearest long record station to each ZIP; airport gust records are screened for sensor spikes, and ridge tops and canyons blow far harder than any airport. Fire zones are the State Fire Marshal's maps; structures destroyed are CAL FIRE damage inspections, which begin in 2013. B

FAIR Plan residential policies and their growth, policies not renewed, and how insurers rate wildfire risk. The retreat of the voluntary market is what sells fire hardened roofs.

FAIR Plan counts are the Plan's own residential policies in force at its fiscal year end (2026); non-renewals are the Department of Insurance's voluntary market file, 2023 being the newest year published, which no longer separates insurer from owner decisions. A region where the Plan grows and insurers rate many homes High or Extreme is where a Class A roof, ember resistant vents and the paperwork for an insurer's discount are a reason to re-roof before the roof wears out. Replacement, repair and tile relay rest on the roof stock and the roof age model; wildfire work rests on CAL FIRE damage inspections; fire hardening, solar, metal, commercial and coating lines lean on assumptions (grade D), so read their columns as relative exposure only.

A market is listed under a region when a city in its name lies in one of the region's counties; the cities in brackets are the ones that do when not all of them do. Which market each county belongs to is set by Nielsen and no county list is in the evidence behind this atlas, so none is given: a region with no market of its own (Sierra and Orange County) is bought from its neighbors, and Medford and Klamath Falls and Reno reach counties on the Oregon and Nevada lines from outside the state. Check the county list with the station or the platform before buying. The cost factor is an assumption of the cost model (grade D). Languages are speakers at home as a share of residents aged 5 and over (ACS).

Licenses are the Contractors State License Board's active C-39 Roofing licenses by mailing address: where a firm is, not where it works. Roofing employers are County Business Patterns establishments with payroll; the Census withholds small county cells, so a region sums only the counties published (8 of 58 counties are withheld). Wind and rain by neighborhood	Airports and a few hundred long record stations stand for whole valleys. Most wind, snow and heat events are filed by forecast zone with no coordinates, and the Santa Ana index stops in 2018.	C

The 11 regions are groups of whole counties chosen for this atlas by climate, housing and media market; they are not a state agency's scheme. Roof stock, roof ages, replacements and rebuild roofs sum the county tables of the roof age model (module 17); FEMA registrations, licenses, employers and languages sum by county; model pools sum the scored ZIPs, each assigned to the county holding most of its land. Weather, fire zone, tile and insurance rate measures are ZIP columns averaged with housing units as the weight (one unit roofs for the roof shares); structures destroyed are ZIP sums. FAIR Plan and non-renewal totals come from the insurance rollup by region when the data carries it, which counts every postal ZIP the source places in the county. A region's roofing character lists the measures on which it stands first or second among the regions, so it changes when the data does. A measure whose column is missing from the data is left out of every table.
