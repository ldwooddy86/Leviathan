READ THIS FIRST: WHAT THE DESK DOES AND DOES NOT DO
Choose a service line (or a blend), a budget and a market scope. The desk picks ZIPs from the paid model, splits the money across Google Search, Local Services Ads, YouTube and Demand Gen, Meta, Microsoft and TikTok, paces it to the line's season, forecasts clicks, leads and sold jobs from assumptions you can edit, and writes what each platform expects: location targets with bid modifiers, keywords with match types and negatives, responsive search ads with the city filled in, social and video copy, and build sheets. Every ad set carries the company name, the CSLB license number and an office city, because California requires the license number in all forms of advertising (Business and Professions Code 7030.5). Nothing offers to waive or absorb a deductible (Penal Code 551), promises to negotiate a claim (Insurance Code 15006, 15007) or promises an insurance discount; copy has no hyphens or dashes. It connects to no platform, pulls no keyword volumes and spends no Ahrefs units. Exports set every object to paused. Fill the Company panel (top right) first: the desk refuses to invent a license number.

The search base here is the service line model's base cost per click ($38 by default, module 12): one number, not two. Search costs multiply by each line's click cost multiplier (module 12) and each ZIP's relative cost (module 05). They also scale by module 12's current base cost per click, and lead rates by each line's click to lead rate, against the model defaults; Apply in module 15 sets those to the account's observed values, and the Meta and Local Services forecasts then use the observed cost per lead of the plan's lines. A cost or rate you change in this table is used as you set it, without that scaling or replacement (the forecast reads cost and lead CVR for search, the cost per lead for Local Services, and cost, CTR and lead CVR for YouTube, Meta and TikTok); Reset to defaults hands it back to the model. Sold jobs use each line's lead to sale rate; social lead forms (Meta, TikTok) close at half that rate and video leads at 60%, because form leads carry more unqualified contacts.
