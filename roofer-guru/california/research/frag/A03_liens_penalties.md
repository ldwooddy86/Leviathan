## A3. Mechanics liens, unlicensed contracting, disasters and price gouging

### A3a. Mechanics lien and preliminary notice basics

| Fact | Value | Short quote | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who must give a preliminary notice | Subcontractors and suppliers, to the owner, the direct contractor and any construction lender. A roofer hired directly by the owner gives it only to a construction lender, if there is one. Labourers give none | "before recording a lien claim, giving a stop payment notice, or asserting a claim against a payment bond, a claimant shall give preliminary notice" and "(1) A laborer is not required to give preliminary notice. (2) A claimant with a direct contractual relationship with an owner or reputed owner is required to give preliminary notice only to the construction lender or reputed construction lender, if any." | {{WB:CIV:8200}}. History line: "Added by Stats. 2010, Ch. 697, Sec. 20. (SB 189) Effective January 1, 2011. Operative July 1, 2012" | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Deadline | Within 20 days of first furnishing work. A late notice only protects work from 20 days before it was served | "A preliminary notice shall be given not later than 20 days after the claimant has first furnished work on the work of improvement." and "only for work performed within 20 days prior to the service of the preliminary notice, and at any time thereafter." | {{WB:CIV:8204}} | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Without it | No valid lien | "Compliance with this section is a necessary prerequisite to the validity of a lien claim or stop payment notice under this title." | Civil Code § 8200(c), same capture | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Recording deadline, contractor hired by the owner | After completing the contract and before the earlier of 90 days after completion of the work, or 60 days after the owner records a notice of completion or cessation | "(a) Ninety days after completion of the work of improvement. (b) Sixty days after the owner records a notice of completion or cessation." | {{WB:CIV:8412}} | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Recording deadline, subcontractors and suppliers | Earlier of 90 days after completion, or 30 days after a recorded notice of completion or cessation | "(1) Ninety days after completion of the work of improvement. (2) Thirty days after the owner records a notice of completion or cessation." | {{WB:CIV:8414}} | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Deadline to sue on the lien | 90 days after recording, or the lien expires | "The claimant shall commence an action to enforce a lien within 90 days after recordation of the claim of lien. If the claimant does not commence an action to enforce the lien within that time, the claim of lien expires and is unenforceable." | {{WB:CIV:8460}} | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Notice to the owner | A copy of the claim with the "NOTICE OF MECHANICS LIEN" must be served on the owner | "A copy of the claim of mechanics lien, which includes the Notice of Mechanics Lien required by paragraph (8) of subdivision (a), shall be served on the owner or reputed owner." | {{WB:CIV:8416}} | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| What the homeowner is told in every contract | The "Mechanics Lien Warning": unpaid subcontractors and suppliers can lien the home even if the contractor was paid | "Even if you pay your contractor in full, unpaid subcontractors, suppliers, and laborers who helped to improve your property may record mechanics liens and sue you in court to foreclose the lien." | Bus. & Prof. Code § 7159(e)(4) (capture cited in A2) | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Lien releases | On request, the contractor must give an unconditional release for the part already paid; the owner may withhold further payment until it is given | "the contractor shall, if requested, obtain and furnish to the person a full and unconditional release ... |---|---|---|---|---|
| Criminal penalty, first conviction | Misdemeanour: fine up to $5,000, or up to six months in county jail, or both | "A first conviction for the offense described in this section is punishable by a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars ($5,000) or by imprisonment in a county jail not exceeding six months, or by both that fine and imprisonment." | {{WB:BPC:7028}}. History line: "Amended by Stats. 2014, Ch. 392, Sec. 3. (SB 315) Effective January 1, 2015." | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Second conviction | Fine of 20 percent of the contract price or $5,000, whichever is greater, and at least 90 days in jail | "the court shall impose a fine of 20 percent of the contract price, or 20 percent of the aggregate payments made to, or at the direction of, the unlicensed person, or five thousand dollars ($5,000), whichever is greater, and ... the person shall be confined in a county jail for not less than 90 days" | Same | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Third and later | Fine $5,000 up to the greater of $10,000 or 20 percent of the contract price; jail 90 days to one year | "a fine of not less than five thousand dollars ($5,000) nor more than the greater amount of ten thousand dollars ($10,000) or 20 percent of the contract price ... and by imprisonment in a county jail for not more than one year or less than 90 days." | Same | VERIFIED (archive capture) |

### A3c. Declared disasters

| Fact | Value | Short quote | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unlicensed contracting on disaster damage | May be charged as a felony: fine up to $10,000, or 16 months, two or three years, or both; or as a misdemeanour: fine up to $1,000 or up to one year in county jail | "for damage or destruction caused by a natural disaster for which a state of emergency is proclaimed by the Governor ... or for which an emergency or major disaster is declared by the President of the United States, shall be punished by a fine up to ten thousand dollars ($10,000), or by imprisonment pursuant to subdivision (h) of Section 1170 of the Penal Code for 16 months, or for two or three years, or by both that fine and imprisonment, or by a fine up to one thousand dollars ($1,000), or by imprisonment in a county jail not exceeding one year" | {{WB:BPC:7028.16}}. History line: "Amended by Stats. 2020, Ch. 364, Sec. 1. (SB 1189) Effective January 1, 2021." public.law shows the same text, "updated Jan. 1, 2021" | VERIFIED (archive capture) |
| Price gouging: materials and goods | For 30 days after a federal, state or local emergency declaration, no more than 10 percent above the pre-emergency price for goods including building materials | "for a period of 30 days following that proclamation or declaration, it is unlawful for a person, contractor, business, or other entity to sell or offer to sell any consumer food items or goods, goods or services used for emergency cleanup, emergency supplies, medical supplies, home heating oil, building materials, housing ... for a price of more than 10 percent greater than the price charged by that person for those goods or services immediately prior to the proclamation or declaration of emergency" | {{PL:PEN:396}}, subdivision (b) | VERIFIED (transcription host) |
| Price gouging: contractors' repair services | For 180 days, no more than 10 percent above the contractor's own pre-emergency price | "for a period of 180 days following that proclamation or declaration, it is unlawful for a contractor to sell or offer to sell any repair or reconstruction services or any services used in emergency cleanup for a price of more than 10 percent above the price charged by that person for those services immediately prior to the proclamation or declaration of emergency." | Penal Code § 396(c), same | VERIFIED (transcription host) |
| The cost pass-through defence | A larger increase is lawful if it is directly attributable to higher labour or material costs and the price is no more than 10 percent above cost plus the contractor's customary markup | "a greater price increase is not unlawful if that person can prove that the increase in price was directly attributable to additional costs imposed on it by the supplier of the goods, or directly attributable to additional costs for labor or materials used to provide the services ... and the price represents no more than 10 percent greater than the total of the cost to the contractor plus the markup customarily applied by the contractor" | Same | VERIFIED (transcription host) |
| Extensions | The Governor, the Legislature or a local body can extend the periods; each local extension is at most 30 days | "The prohibitions of this section may be extended for additional periods, as needed ... Each extension by a local legislative body or local official shall not exceed 30 days." | Penal Code § 396(g) | VERIFIED (transcription host) |
| Penalty | Misdemeanour: up to one year in county jail, fine up to $10,000, or both; also unfair competition | "A violation of this section is a misdemeanor punishable by imprisonment in a county jail for a period not exceeding one year, by a fine of not more than ten thousand dollars ($10,000), or by both that fine and imprisonment." | Penal Code § 396(h) | VERIFIED (transcription host) |
| Fraud in disaster repair | A felony theft or forgery conviction as part of a scheme to defraud an owner over disaster repairs carries an extra year | "shall receive a one-year enhancement in addition and consecutive to the penalty prescribed." | {{PL:PEN:667.16}} | VERIFIED (transcription host) |
