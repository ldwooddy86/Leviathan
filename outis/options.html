Outis

Sites see nobody, from nowhere, at no particular time.

Outis is on

Every site you open now gets less. Trackers don't load, your device looks like a common one, location requests are refused, and the clock sites see says UTC instead of your time zone. Two choices are worth making now:

The time zone sites see

Your time zone tells sites roughly where you live. Hiding it means some sites will show times in UTC.

When you close a site

Forgetting deletes its cookies, so it can't tell you've been there before. You'll be signed out of it, except on sites you keep.

You can change any of this later on this page, and pause Outis on any site from its toolbar button.

Who you are

What lets sites, and the companies behind their trackers, recognize you from site to site.

Block trackers

Analytics, ad networks, social media pixels, fingerprinting and identity services, session recorders: on the list. Also turns off Chrome's ad topics, ad measurement and link pings.

Block third-party cookies

Stops services embedded in other sites from storing anything that follows you between them. A few embedded sign-ins and comment boxes may need a pause.

Clean tracking tags from links

Removes utm_ campaign tags, fbclid, gclid and other identifiers from addresses before they're opened.

Tell sites not to sell or share my data

Sends Global Privacy Control, which many businesses are legally required to honor as an opt-out under California's privacy law and several other states'.

Mask my device fingerprint

Canvas and graphics

Adds noise you can't see to what sites read back from drawings, different on every site and every browser session, and reports a common graphics card.

Audio

Adds noise you can't hear to what sites read back from sound processing.

Device details

Reports 8 CPU cores and 8 GB of memory, and hides your exact OS version, hardware model, connection speed, installed voices, keyboard layout, fonts and disk size.

Screen

Reports your window as your screen, and hides where the window sits across your monitors.

Where you are

What gives away your location.

Where sites think you are

Sets the time zone sites see and, if you choose to pretend below, the location they get.

When a site asks for my location

Pick a place above to pretend to be there. "Nowhere" has no location, so requests are refused.

Look like an English (US) browser

Sends en-US as your language to sites and scripts, so your language settings don't point to a region. Sites may offer English first.

Keep WebRTC from revealing my addresses

Video-call technology can expose your network addresses, and your real address even behind a proxy. With a proxy on, WebRTC must go through it.

Don't tell sites where I came from

When you go from one site to another, the new one isn't told which site sent you.

Your IP address

Every site sees the address your connection comes from. It shows roughly where you are, usually your city or region, and who your internet provider is. Outis can't change it by itself. A VPN app or Tor does, or send your traffic through a proxy here.

Send my traffic through a proxy

For a proxy you run or subscribe to, or Tor (start Tor Browser, then use 127.0.0.1 port 9150). Sites then see the proxy's address.

The user name and password are for HTTP and HTTPS proxies (Chrome can't sign in to SOCKS proxies). They stay in this browser's extension storage.

When you're there

What tells sites your local time, your daily rhythm, and that you've been before.

Hide my clock and time zone

Sites see the time in UTC instead of yours. Some will show times in that zone.

Hide when I'm active

Refuses idle detection (sites asking whether you're at the computer) and reports a full, charging battery, so battery level can't be used to link your visits.

Forget sites when I close them

When the last tab of a site closes, its cookies and stored data are deleted, so next time it can't tell you've been there. You'll be signed out, except on sites you keep (below, or from the toolbar button).

      What Outis can't hide

      Honest limits, so you know what else to do.

      Your IP address
      Every site sees it, and it points to your area and your internet provider. Use a VPN, Tor, or the proxy setting above.
      Accounts you sign in to
      A site you sign in to knows who you are. Outis stops it following you elsewhere, not recognizing you on its own pages.
      The moment you visit
      A site always knows when your request reached it. Outis hides your clock, and forgetting stops it linking one visit to the next.
      Background scripts
      Scripts a page runs in background workers are out of Outis's reach and still see your real time zone and device details. Few trackers use them, but some fingerprinting scripts do.
      Every last fingerprint
      Outis covers the signals trackers use most. Your window size, your fonts (measured from drawn text) and many small details stay visible, so a determined fingerprinting service can still sometimes recognize a returning browser.
      Trackers on the site itself
      Tracking that a site serves from its own domain, or disguises as its own, isn't on the list.
      Fraud checks
      Banks and stores run device checks at sign-in and checkout. Outis leaves those services alone, because blocking them gets you locked out. If a site still refuses you, pause Outis there.
      Chrome and Google
      Chrome itself, a signed-in Google account with sync, and other extensions are outside what Outis controls.

      Outis sends nothing anywhere. The only request it ever makes on its own is "Check what sites see", and only when you click it.
